[A Reunion with the Past]
Stony Brook University 
Staller Center for the Arts 

This morning, I went to a farm on Long Island with some friends. Since Stony Brook was nearby, I decided to bring them back to my alma mater and walk around the art building.

I originally just wanted to see whether the master-copy projects our graduating class painted on the walls back in 2012 were still there...

To my surprise, not only was my painting still there—it had even been framed by someone afterward!

And then came an even bigger coincidence:

My former department chair, professor, and advisor, Martin Levine, somehow happened to be in the art building on a Sunday afternoon—and I ran into him!!

—

Seeing Mr. Levine again after all these years made me incredibly happy and excited!

Worried that he might not remember exactly who I was... I quickly introduced myself and told him I was Jones.

He said he still remembered me, and then started sharing stories and reflections from his recent trip to China, where he had held a solo exhibition!

He told me that he was preparing to retire. He and his assistant were sorting through his office and getting ready to move everything out by the end of the month.

But, as he put it:

“I’ll still be around...”

So apparently, he will still be teaching occasionally and showing up around the department.

—

After briefly catching up, he walked with my friends and me to see the painting I had left on the wall.

And yes—it really was still there!

I guess artists really do understand how to respect other people’s work... even though nearly all the details on its face have fallen off by now. XD

Fourteen years after graduating, I returned to my alma mater, unexpectedly reunited with my professor, and took a photo with him in front of the final piece I created there as a student.

What an incredibly meaningful image!

I was deeply moved.

—

Finally, he asked what I was doing now.

I told him that I had eventually become a software engineer and now spend my days writing code...

He replied:

“Smart decision.”

We both laughed. XD

But I also added that, although I did not continue down the path of art, what I learned during university has continued to influence me all these years.

I never truly stopped creating.

Only the form changed.

Writing, video editing, code—and even the enormous amount of natural-language thinking tokens I now generate every day while going back and forth with AI...

Each of these, in its own way, still feels like a form of creation to me.

And they all began here.

Perhaps that painting, still hanging on the wall, was also reminding me:

The part of myself that was shaped here never truly left.

Fourteen years later, just as my professor is preparing to retire, I somehow found myself standing in front of it again—reunited with him and taking a photo together...

Sometimes, life really does know how to arrange a warm ending.

________

上午跟朋友去長島農場玩，剛好附近是 Stony Brook，就順便帶他們回我的母校藝術系館走走。

本只是想確認一下，2012 年畢業那屆，大家在牆上畫仿畫的 projects 作品是否依然建在……

沒想到不只還在，竟還被後人給裱框起來！

更巧的是，以前的系主任、我的教授兼導師 Martin Levine，在這禮拜天的下午竟還待在系館，被我巧遇！！

—

事隔多年再見到 Mr. Levine，令我十分欣喜與激動！

為了怕老師忘記我是哪位……我趕緊主動報上名，說自己是 Jones。

老師說他還記得我，並開始跟我分享自己近期出訪中國舉辦個展的見聞跟心得！

老師說，他正準備退休，此時正和助教一同整理辦公室，準備月底搬出。

但他說自己：

will still be around...

（還是會兼課出沒）

—

簡單寒暄聊了近況以後，老師陪我和友人一同走去看了我牆上的畫。

確實還在！

說明藝術人果然還是懂得尊重作品……儘管它面部的細節幾乎都掉光了……XD

畢業後事隔 14 年，回到母校並意外的和老師重逢，又在自己當年的最後一副作品前合影。

多麼具有意義的畫面！

我十分感動！

—

最後，老師問我現在在做什麼？

我說自己後來轉職成了軟體工程師，每天寫 code……

他回：

「Smart decision」

我們都笑了……XD

不過我也補充的說，雖然沒有繼續走在藝術這條路上，但大學時期的所學，對我的影響其實一直都在。

我從未真的停止創作，只是形式改變了。

文字寫作、影音剪輯、乃至於程式碼，甚至於是現在每天大量的自然語言和 AI 切磋的思維 token 輸出……

這些的一種種，我認為都還是一種創作的形式。

它們都起源與此！

或許那幅被留在牆上的畫，也像是在提醒我：

當年在這裡養成的那一部分自己，其實從未離開。

十四年後，在老師即將退休之際，又恰好回到它的面前，與老師重逢合影……

只能說，人生偶爾真的很懂得安排一個溫暖的收尾。
